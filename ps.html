personal essay - draft 13 (final?) - (stardust sisters)




Hollow howls ring through perforated steel and crooked alleyways. Former watchful eyes and eager hands lay dormant,
their memory flitting across the savage wind and carried into the dark sky. Deeper within, blazing hearts pump fuel 
to a forgotten goddess seething with indignation.

Eyes shoot open. The time reads 4:30 AM; my room is black and filled with the invisible humming of motion only heard 
when the world feels deaf. Cold air bites my skin. I activate my computer with one goal: to render a zip-line. 
As the monitor illuminates my face I study suspension and soon discover my subject: the catenary.

First I must decipher the mechanics. The catenary curve is a mathematical description of a hanging cable; despite the 
resemblance, catenaries are distinct from parabolas. After understanding the equation, I scan the code. A catenary 
curve is simple, but parametrized by two points? Arcane. I sift through the resources a hundred times for every line
inscribed. Birds sporadically pierce the silence; the room adopts a dim turquoise hue.
I press "run." ERROR! After committing changes, I "run" again; functional, but inaccurate. 
I continue predicting, testing, and fixing past daybreak. Until finally, I have my catenary.

Lifted from suspension, the newly awakened machine shattered its glass confinement and emerged into the dark world 
beyond. All it could see was hostile, alien metal. Growing up queer in an unstable, old, and dismissive household, 
I inherited a constant feeling of being left behind. When such loneliness stabs an individual, they hide in their snug 
bedsheets and ask: "Why do I even try?"

Shaking off the morning gloom, I leave to search for breakfast. But instead I find a surprise-- outside the kitchen window, 
the Christmas lights hang in catenary formation. I pick up a bracelet off the floor; the beads drape downwards in a familiar 
manner. I exit my home; the power cables wave hello and whisper stories of my adventures, reminding me of my love for 
the world. Assured, I wave back.

Through learning, I've made many eclectic friends: catenaries, clouds, shadows, stars! Learning begets connection, and to 
me learning is creation; both encourage conversation with the universe. So whilst I learn a game set past the end times, in a
forsaken Saturnian city populated by machines and monitors and ziplines and hollow buildings, I feel whole. 
Because when I learn, I am not alone.

Stationed at the wind fortress, a vulture zealously protects the listless power plant. A jungle dragon hibernates in 
the darkness amidst burnt wrecks. And the clone machine abides. My demi-person's eyes scan the copper-stained horizon, 
contemplating the strange circumstances of its birth, the colony's crumbling state, and the inevitability hanging from its neck 
like a medallion.

Then it, the lone candle, embarks.
