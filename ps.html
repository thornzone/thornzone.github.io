personal essay - draft 11 (final?) - (steelsmoke)




Hollow howls ring through the perforated steel and crooked alleyways. Former watchful eyes and eager hands lay dormant,
their memory flitting through the savage wind and carried into the dark sky. Deeper within, blazing hearts pump fuel 
to a forgotten goddess seething with indignation.

My eyes shoot open. The time reads 4:30 AM; the room is black and filled with the invisible sounds of motion only heard wh
en the world feels deaf. It is cold. I turn on my computer with one goal. I sprite a golden hook, and place two;
one at the level's edge, the other across the clouds. To properly render my zipline between them I research suspensio-
n, and thus discover the catenary.

First, I must decipher the mechanics. The catenary curve is a mathematical description of a hanging cable; despite the resem-
blance, catenaries are distinct from parabolas. Architecture makes heavy use of catenaries; Thomas Jefferson first coined the 
word when referencing arches. Neat! Once I understand the equation, I look at the code. Two birds' piercing and unpredictable 
chirps relieve the silence; the room takes a dark turquoise hue.A catenary curve is simple, but defined by two points? Arcane. 
I sift through the resources a hundred times for every line inscribed. Once satisfactory; I press "run." Error! After committing
changes, I "run" again. Functional, but inaccurate. After writing, fixing, stopping, and thinking--by now chatter from the radio 
travels to my room and basks in daylight; my father listens when he thinks nobody else is awake--I have my catenary. Except it's 
still a little broken.

Roaming around, I do not see power cables. I spy their mechanical equilibria; I recall the countless
hours I spent studying and tinkering to construct a zipline, and then I remember myself-- the love I have for what I learn 
and create. I see a friend. 

As someone who perpetually feels alone, my eclectic stardust sisters--catenaries, clouds, shadows, space-- grant solace. Growing up in an unstable 
household with people much older than me, I inherited a constant feeling of being left behind. When such doubt permeates an individual's character, they 
hide in their warm, snug bedsheets and ask questions. "Why do I try to improve?"

My slender friend teaches the value of learning. Connection. Learning begets teaching and teaching begets learning
and both connect. Learning is creation, because both encourage conversation with the universe. So when I 
learn a video game set in a forsaken Saturnian city only populated by the oppression of a collective structure,
with robots and fire and steel and smoke, I feel whole. Because I am not alone.

Since then, curiosity (or creativity) not only connected me to the universe, but also brought me closer to humble and kind people; a community I am proud to be part of-- 
game development is no longer sitting in a cold, dark room and talking to myself. (Well, sometimes I still do. Programming is hard!)
Instead, I bounce ideas off of friends, share goofy art of our characters, discuss potential story beats, and listen to their music.
    
Stationed at the wind fortress, a vulture zealously protected the listless power plant. A jungle dragon hibernated in 
the darkness amidst burnt wrecks. And the clone-machine abided. My demi-person's eyes scanned the copper-stained horizon, 
pondering the strange circumstances of its birth, the colony's crumbling state, and the inevitability hanging from its neck 
like a medallion.

Then it, the lone candle, embarked.
